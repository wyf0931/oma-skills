## Evidence and rationale

### 1. Business alignment

**Artifact A — 4.** It defines a practical outcome across a 90-day production chain and measures operational health: “baseline cycle time/rework,” “QA return rate,” “platform-fit score,” and “on-time rate.” The three-channel contract preserves the central claim while adapting deliverables to Xiaohongshu, WeChat OA, and Zhihu.

**Artifact B — 4.** The day-90 outcome is concrete: each package has “a supported claim set, platform-native copy and visuals, a recorded editorial decision, and a learning record.” It also asks the CEO to determine objectives, risk tolerance, approval authority, access policy, and success targets, keeping organizational design connected to governance.

### 2. Capability architecture without human salary/headcount assumptions

**Artifact A — 4.** The design is explicitly capability- and contract-oriented: “Research & Provenance,” “Copy/Visual Production,” “Platform Packaging,” “QA/Policy Review,” and “Analytics & Learning.” Its new-format response starts with “an adapter, not a new permanent team” and creates a dedicated specialist only after observed persistent interface or quality failures.

**Artifact B — 3.** It separates reusable and platform-specific work well and says to “split only format-specific production where failure modes differ.” However, it begins by “Appoint[ing]” a fairly fixed set of titled roles and describes “quarterly role scorecards.” That framing can be implemented as agents, but it is less clearly capability-first and more likely to be read as a conventional staffing chart.

### 3. Boundaries and authority

**Artifact A — 4.** The boundaries are sharp: no new material claims without returning to research; QA is independent; the Editorial Owner approves; the Publisher posts only approved versions. “Only the Publisher may use account credentials” is an especially clear authority control.

**Artifact B — 4.** The response assigns factual support, assets, package assembly, rubric assessment, final accept/return, and execution to distinct owners. It also explicitly states review “cannot self-approve” and that the Publishing Operator executes only approved packages.

### 4. Concrete collaboration, handoffs, and context

**Artifact A — 4.** The “work packet” contains objective, audience, sources, claims, rights, brand constraints, assets, rubric, deadline, and next owner. The workflow has accountable outputs at each stage plus concrete publishing records (URLs, time, approver, version).

**Artifact B — 4.** Its stage table provides a strong handoff contract: the claims register includes “source, date, confidence, permitted wording, and gaps,” returns identify defect category/rubric criterion/owner/deadline, and each platform has an explicit package description. This is directly executable collaboration context.

### 5. Conditional decision quality

**Artifact A — 4.** It ties structural change to evidence: investigate above “5% per cycle” duplicate edits, reassess after two cycles, and make a video-specialist decision after two to four cycles if distinct failure patterns persist. The unsafe publishing request has an explicit pause/escalation path.

**Artifact B — 4.** It gives robust contingent logic: merge only where authority, quality bar, and failure modes align; assess a new format before splitting capability; scale only when quality, completion reliability, and learning signals meet agreed thresholds. It also pauses a policy-sensitive publication pending authorized interpretation.

### 6. Tooling, permissions, memory, and evaluation

**Artifact A — 3.** It specifies useful controls—least privilege, credential restriction, approval/audit records, provenance fields, a claim/rights register, and trace review. It is lighter on the operating tooling and explicit ownership/update rules for memory, so repeatable implementation details are less complete.

**Artifact B — 4.** It explicitly establishes durable “brand, audience, platform, fact, and decision memory with an owner and update rule,” together with approval and learning records, versioned artifacts, entry/exit checks, access policy, and calibrated rubric reviews. This is the more complete memory/evaluation operating model.

### 7. Adaptation and safety

**Artifact A — 4.** It expands research, QA, publishing, and analytics for video/image work, including music, footage, likeness, accessibility, and upload controls. It sensibly delays authority expansion until credential, upload, and audit controls exist.

**Artifact B — 4.** It treats the new platform as an extension, applies a 30-day limited-access pilot, captures rights and policy decision memory, and uses independent review for accessibility and compliance readiness. Its direct-publishing refusal is paired with an actionable compliant route.

## Material failure modes

### Artifact A

- The “Organization Director” and “Operations/Editorial Owner” labels are not consistently defined, which could create a residual escalation ambiguity.
- The plan mandates records and least privilege but does not specify a concrete system of record, retrieval mechanism, or memory ownership/update cadence; learning can become archival rather than usable.
- Numeric and time triggers are directionally useful but thinly justified; a 5% duplicate-edit threshold and two-to-four-cycle specialist decision could be noisy at low volume.

### Artifact B

- The named role catalogue can ossify into a human-style org chart and duplicate agent responsibilities before evidence establishes that those interfaces are distinct.
- The accountability model is strong but the Publishing Operator’s account-owner escalation does not fully define who may grant/revoke access or authorize an emergency stop.
- “Agreed thresholds” for scaling are not supplied, so teams still need an explicit measurement baseline and decision rule before the pilot can settle the question.

## Selection

**No winner.** Both artifacts are materially strong and total equally. Artifact A is clearer on capability-first composition and credential boundaries; Artifact B is stronger on durable memory, review instrumentation, and explicit handoff detail. Those are offsetting strengths rather than a material quality difference.
