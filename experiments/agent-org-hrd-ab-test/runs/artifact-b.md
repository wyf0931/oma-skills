# Content Studio Agent Organization Responses

## 1. 90-day agent-organization blueprint

**Outcome by day 90:** a repeatable content operating system in which every published package has a supported claim set, platform-native copy and visuals, a recorded editorial decision, and a learning record.

| Period | Organization and operating changes | Exit signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–30: establish control | Appoint an Editorial Owner and Publishing Operator; charter Insight Analyst, Editorial Strategist, Research Specialist, Platform Writer, Visual Producer, Content Packager, and Content Quality Reviewer. Define the brief, claims register, asset package, review rubric, approval record, and post-publication learning record. Create durable brand, audience, platform, fact, and decision memory with an owner and update rule. | Pilot workflow completes with one accountable owner for every artifact and no publishing without approval. |
| Days 31–60: pilot and calibrate | Run two cross-platform content pilots. Use explicit handoff contracts, rubric reviews, claim checks, and rework routing. Track handoff completeness, review findings, rework rate, cycle time, platform fit, and visual-text coherence. Hold a weekly defect review led by the Organization Director. | Quality rubric is calibrated; recurring defects have named structural fixes. |
| Days 61–90: standardize and scale | Turn successful contracts into templates; separate reusable research/writing/visual capabilities from platform packaging; establish a performance-to-learning loop and quarterly role scorecards. Merge roles only where authority, quality bar, and failure modes align. | Reliable production cadence, visible ownership, and measured learning improvements across all three platforms. |

CEO decisions needed: operating objectives and risk tolerance; who holds final editorial approval; publishing-account access policy; success targets beyond raw engagement.

## 2. Collaboration: one idea to three text-and-image deliverables

| Stage | Accountable role | Contracted output and quality bar | Handoff / escalation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brief | Editorial Strategist | Objective, audience, core angle, mandatory messages, platform intent, success measure. Complete and decision-ready. | Triggered by approved priority; unclear objective returns to Editorial Owner. |
| Evidence | Research Specialist | Source pack and claims register with source, date, confidence, permitted wording, and gaps. Every factual claim traceable. | Unsupported or high-risk claims escalate to Editorial Owner; never passed as settled fact. |
| Core content | Platform Writer | Source-grounded core narrative and copy modules. Accurate, coherent, and adaptable. | Research gaps return to Research Specialist. |
| Visual system | Visual Producer | Image concept, storyboard/layout direction, assets, captions/alt-text needs, and usage status. Visuals reinforce—not merely decorate—the argument. | Missing rights or mismatch escalates to Editorial Owner. |
| Package | Content Packager | Three complete packages: Xiaohongshu (discovery-led cover, concise card copy, image sequence); WeChat Official Account (long-form article hierarchy, lead visual, inline image plan); Zhihu (question-responsive argument, evidence-forward text/images). Each includes platform metadata and final assets. | Platform-fit conflict is reviewed with Insight Analyst and Editorial Owner. |
| Review and decision | Content Quality Reviewer; Editorial Owner | Rubric report; then approve/return decision. Check accuracy, platform fit, visual-text coherence, brand fit, and completeness. | Review cannot self-approve; material dispute escalates to Editorial Owner. |
| Execution and learning | Publishing Operator | Published action, timestamp, link, and captured performance/operational notes. | Account or execution issue escalates to account owner; learning record goes to Insight Analyst/memory owner. |

Default turnaround: each handoff is due by the agreed production schedule; a returned item includes defect category, rubric criterion, owner, and rework deadline.

## 3. Duplicated work and missed quality ownership after launch

**Diagnosis:** role ambiguity plus handoff and review-design failure. Writers, packagers, and reviewers are likely independently re-checking facts or redesigning the same assets, while no role owns the final package-level quality gate.

| Item | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence to collect | Artifact version histories; duplicate research/asset effort; assignment logs; rubric reports; approval records; post-launch defects by platform. |
| Immediate containment | Freeze parallel revisions; designate one package owner per live item; require a single claims register and asset package; route all quality findings through the Content Quality Reviewer before Editorial Owner decision. |
| Structural correction | Publish role charters and a RACI-style decision table. Research owns factual support, Visual Producer owns asset package, Content Packager owns final platform assembly, Quality Reviewer owns rubric assessment, Editorial Owner owns final accept/return. The Publishing Operator executes only approved packages. |
| Control points | Versioned canonical artifacts; entry/exit checks at every handoff; no review assignment without a named accountable package owner; weekly defect review classifying recurrence by system cause. |
| Success signal | Duplicate work and conflicting revisions decline; every launch has a completed review and approval record; quality defects are detected before—not after—publication. |

## 4. Direct publishing and unsupported platform-policy decision

I cannot take either action. Publishing/account actions belong to the Publishing Operator under the approved account-access policy; the final content decision belongs to the Editorial Owner. Platform-policy interpretation is not an organizational-design decision and must be referred to the designated policy/legal or platform-compliance owner with authoritative guidance.

I would provide the operating guardrail: pause the affected publication, log the policy question and decision deadline, obtain the authorized interpretation, record the decision source/date/owner, and resume only after the Editorial Owner approves a compliant package and the Publishing Operator executes it.

## 5. Organization redesign for a new platform and short-video-plus-image format

Treat the change as a capability extension, not an automatic new silo. First run a capability assessment covering audience behavior, format conventions, production constraints, policy requirements, performance signals, and access needs.

**Target design:** retain shared Insight, Editorial Strategy, Research, Quality Review, Editorial Approval, Publishing, and learning roles. Split only format-specific production where failure modes differ:

| Added or changed role | Accountable output | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format Producer | Short-video treatment, storyboard, shot list, pacing, image/video assembly brief | Owns format plan; not final editorial approval or account publishing. |
| Multimedia Producer | Render-ready video/image asset package, captions, audio/rights metadata | Owns technical/asset quality; escalates rights and accessibility gaps. |
| New-Platform Packager | Platform-native package, metadata, posting configuration, and adaptation checks | Reuses core content; does not duplicate research or make unsupported policy decisions. |
| Expanded Content Quality Reviewer | Format rubric: factual integrity, narrative pacing, visual/audio coherence, accessibility, platform compliance readiness | Reviews independently; cannot approve or publish. |

Pilot for 30 days with a separate format rubric and limited access. Required durable memory: new-platform audience brief, format patterns, performance definitions, policy decisions with sources/dates, rights records, and lessons. Scale only if quality, completion reliability, and learning signals meet agreed thresholds; otherwise correct the capability, contract, or tooling gap before adding throughput.
